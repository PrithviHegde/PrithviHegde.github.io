Question
Why do plants spread faster than an average dispersal distance predicts? (Reid's paradox: oak reclaimed Britain far too fast after the last ice age.)
Method
Simulated spread under Gaussian, exponential and lognormal dispersal kernels with the same mean distance; modelled when ferns appear with Weibull waiting times.
Result
After 40 generations the fat-tailed lognormal colony reaches 14× farther than the Gaussian one, and it is still speeding up.

FilingDelta In progress

2026–

Question
When management changes the wording of a company's SEC filings, does the stock price react, and how quickly?
Method
Extract the Management's Discussion & Analysis from 88 filings of 11 large caps, match each paragraph to its earlier version with sentence embeddings, and have a local LLM label every change by topic, materiality and direction. Abnormal returns are timed to when the SEC accepted each filing, with the earnings release measured separately so the two can be told apart.
Status
9,687 paragraph changes found and abnormal returns computed. Labelling the changes with a local LLM, checked against a 50-item gold set labelled by two independent annotators. The price test comes next.

About

Two questions I keep coming back to: When a model explains its decision, do people decide better, or just more confidently? And how do we know when a model's decision should take precedence over our own expertise and judgement?

I first ran into these questions at Wells Fargo, where I built underwriting strategy that changed real lending decisions at scale. Applicants with thin credit files are a good example: the model sometimes gave them a confident-looking score built on very little evidence, and sometimes declined them outright on that same lack of evidence. How do we tell those applicants apart, and know when to route them to human judgement? These are the kinds of questions I tried to answer as an analyst.

Those questions led me to Seattle, and a master's in data science at the University of Washington. Here, I want to build expertise in designing models that are well calibrated, explain their decisions, and learn when to defer to human judgement. I'm looking for internships, and for chances to talk with people working on human-in-the-loop decision problems. Away from work, I'm slowly getting to know the city, and I'm always down for a game of tennis.
